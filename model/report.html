Improvement over the baseline, with 95% CI

What is being sampled: melons. For each of the 25 melons we have two numbers — how far the model missed, and how far the baseline missed. Subtract them and you get that melon’s improvement. Then draw 25 melons with replacement from those 25 (some appear twice, some not at all), average their improvements, and repeat 5,000 times. The middle 95% of those averages is the bar below.

It answers: if we had bought a different 25 melons from the same market, would the model still have beaten guessing? A bar crossing the dashed line means no — not reliably.

Averaging over 20 splits before resampling matters: built on a single split this same test reported +22.7% for primary while the 100-fold table said −13.0%, because that one split happened to be kind. Positive numbers mean the model beat the baseline by that many °Brix.

Permutation null

What is being shuffled: the sweetness values. Take the 25 Brix readings and randomly reassign them to melons — melon 1 gets melon 17’s sweetness. The cue measurements stay with their own melon; only the answers move. Now there is, by construction, no real relationship left. Refit, cross-validate, record the error. Repeat 1000 times.

That builds a picture of what “nothing” scores on your own data, rather than assuming a formula. The grey histogram is those shuffled runs; the coloured line is the real model. p is the fraction of shuffles that did at least as well.

Why this and the bootstrap disagree: they ask different questions. The permutation asks “could this be nothing at all?” — and for primary, probably not (p = 0.023). The bootstrap asks “how big is it, and is it reliably above zero from melon to melon?” — and there the answer is that it cannot be pinned above zero. A small but real effect produces exactly this pattern.

Is ridge the bottleneck?

Six model families, same cross-validation budget as the headline table.

At n=25 the algorithm is not the limiting factor, the data is. Tree models lose to a shrunken linear model, as expected at this size. PLS edges ahead — it is the standard method in fruit near-infrared work — but it was not the preregistered model, so promoting it now would be selection after seeing results.

Caveats

  • n=25: any difference smaller than the fold sd is not a result.
  • The success bar is recomputed from the CROSS-VALIDATED baseline; cues.json's target_mae_15pct came from the in-sample deviation and is an unfairly hard bar for a cross-validated model.
  • The only set under the bar is an ablation variant (minus_shape). Promoting it would be selection after seeing results.
  • No noise floor is available: the three Brix readings are top/middle/bottom of the cut face, so their spread is the radial gradient, not refractometer error.
  • Leave-one-variety-out has only 2 groups, so it is coarse.
  • Cue 2 (weight) and cue 6 (stripe width) were never testable.
